# Video Motion Principles

Use this reference when planning animation video language, shot hierarchy, staging, rhythm, and visible state change.

## Motion Has A Job

Every animated shot should do at least one useful thing:

- Establish: make subject, place, and pressure readable.
- Reveal: disclose hidden information.
- Pressure: increase conflict, danger, desire, or curiosity.
- Turn: change the situation.
- Release: let the viewer understand consequence.
- Button: land a final joke, image, emotion, or question.

If motion does not change attention, emotion, information, or consequence, simplify it.

## Hierarchy

Motion creates hierarchy faster than color or detail:

- Main subject moves first or receives the clearest motion.
- Camera moves only when it improves readability or feeling.
- Background motion stays slower, softer, or lower contrast.
- Effects support contact, scale, energy, or atmosphere.

When everything moves, nothing leads.

## Staging

Good staging makes the action obvious:

- Start from a readable first frame.
- Keep the most important subject separated by silhouette, light, color, distance, or motion.
- Use props and thresholds to show cause and obstacle.
- Let the final frame communicate the new state without extra explanation.

Think in beats:

1. Setup: show current state clearly.
2. Anticipation: let the viewer prepare for action.
3. Action: move or transform the subject.
4. Impact: show contact, reveal, or change.
5. Reaction: body, prop, environment, or sound answers.
6. Settle: land an inheritable ending state.

## Rhythm

Rhythm comes from contrast:

- Fast motion reads better after stillness.
- Emotional turns need holds.
- Dense information needs slower camera or fewer moving parts.
- Action can accelerate, but contact and aftermath still need clarity.
- Sound can mark timing even when the image is complex.

Avoid making all shots the same length and intensity.

## Continuity As Story

Continuity is how the viewer trusts a sequence:

- Character identity stays stable while emotion and damage change.
- Scene identity stays stable while weather, light, traces, and object state evolve.
- Screen direction stays stable unless reversal is the dramatic point.
- Prop ownership and damage carry across shots.
- The next shot inherits the previous shot's visible ending state.

## Sound-Aware Motion

Plan sound even before final audio:

- Anticipation can be marked by tiny sound or sudden silence.
- Impact needs contact sound or deliberate absence.
- Reaction can be carried by breath, cloth, debris, echo, or ambient tail.
- A cut feels sharper when sound leads or stops with intention.

Sound should sharpen the visual beat, not explain what the image failed to show.
